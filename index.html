An improved bound for the exceptional set of the Erdős–Straus equation

Sushaan Kandukoori, Shrikrishna Ramesh, Pranava Kumar

Paper Code BibTeX

Abstract

Let \(E(N)\) be the number of integers \(2\le n\le N\) for which

\[ \frac4n=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z \]

has no solution in positive integers. Vaughan proved \(E(N)\ll N\exp\{-c(\log N)^{2/3}\}\). We prove

\[ E(N)\ll N\exp\{-c(\log N)^{3/4}\}. \]

For each \(a\ge1\), the divisors of \(a^2\) supply congruence classes modulo \(4a-1\) on which such representations exist. For a large parameter \(X\), we select a family whose reciprocal moduli sum to order \((\log X)^3\). Averaging over small divisors retains this weight while controlling shared prime factors. A finite counting argument gives the stated bound at the scale \(\log X\asymp(\log N)^{1/4}\). The same argument gives this exponent for every fixed numerator \(m\ge4\).

Representations of \(4/n\)

Introduction and main theorem

The Erdős–Straus conjecture asserts that for every integer \(n\ge2\) the equation

\[ \frac4n=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z \]

has a solution in positive integers. Let \(E(N)\) count the exceptions \(2\le n\le N\). Vaughan proved the classical estimate

\[ E(N)\ll N\exp\{-c(\log N)^{2/3}\} \]

for some absolute \(c>0\). Our main result improves the exponent.

Theorem 1 (main theorem). There is an absolute constant \(c>0\) such that, for all \(N\ge3\), \[ E(N)\ll N\exp\{-c(\log N)^{3/4}\}. \]

The same saving holds relative to the primes: the number of exceptional primes up to \(N\) is \(\ll\pi(N)\exp\{-c(\log N)^{3/4}\}\) after decreasing \(c\).

For \(m\ge4\), let \(E_m(N)\) count the positive integers \(n\le N\) for which \(m/n\) is not a sum of three unit fractions.

Theorem 2 (Erdős–Straus–Schinzel exceptional sets for a fixed numerator). For each fixed integer \(m\ge4\), there are constants \(c_m,C_m>0\) such that, for all \(N\ge3\), \[ E_m(N)\le C_mN\exp\{-c_m(\log N)^{3/4}\}. \]

The cases \(m=2,3\) have no exceptions, since

\[ \frac2n=\frac1n+\frac1{2n}+\frac1{2n},\qquad \frac3n=\frac1n+\frac1n+\frac1n. \]

Pomerance–Weingartner prove \(E_m(N)\le N\exp\{-C(\log N)^{2/3}/\varphi(m)^{1/3}\}\) uniformly for \(4\le m\le(\log N)^2\). For each fixed \(m\), Theorem 2 improves the exponent from \(2/3\) to \(3/4\), with constants depending on \(m\).

Example 1.1. For \(n=2521\), take \(a=12\) and \(h=16\). Then \(h\mid a^2\) and \[ 4a-1=47,\qquad n+4h=2585=47\cdot55. \] Lemma 2.3 gives \[ \frac4{2521}=\frac1{644}+\frac1{30252}+\frac1{1217643}. \]

We use the Type II congruences

\[ 4a-1\mid n+4h,\qquad h\mid a^2. \]

The choices \(h=1,a,a^2\) give the shifts \(-4,-1,-a\) modulo \(4a-1\), respectively. The sum over the remaining divisors has cubic logarithmic order; see Proposition 4.2.

In the selected family, compatible congruences with the same large prime coincide. We bound the remaining overlaps by sums over common small factors. Two divisor sums give weight \((\log X)^2\); averaging over small divisors supplies \(\log z\), which combines with the rough cofactor sum \(\log X/\log z\) to give the third logarithm.

Remark 1.2 (the exponent \(3/4\)). For a family of congruences with total weight \(\mu\asymp(\log X)^k\) and the overlap estimates below, the level condition of Theorem 5.4 has the form \(\log P(z)+O(\mu\log X)\le\log N\). When \(\log P(z)=O((\log X)^{k+1})\), choosing \(\log X\) of order \((\log N)^{1/(k+1)}\) gives a saving \(\exp\{-c(\log N)^{k/(k+1)}\}\). The family with prime moduli used in the Vaughan method has quadratic mass. Here the cubic mass gives \(3/4\).

Formalization

The companion Lean 4 development proves Theorems 1 and 2, the bound for exceptional primes, Example 1.1, and every numbered result of Sections 2–6, using only Mathlib and Lean's standard axioms. Its proof of Theorem 3.7 adapts an existing open source formalization of the Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem, credited in the repository.

Congruences

Fix \(m\ge4\). A Type II representation for a prime denominator is one in which exactly two denominators are divisible by the prime. The proof of Theorem 1 uses only Lemma 2.3. For arbitrary positive denominators we use the following sufficient condition.

Lemma 2.3 (a sufficient condition). Let \(n\ge1\), let \(a\ge1\), let \(q=ma-1\), and let \(e\) be a positive divisor of \(a^2\). If \[ n\equiv-me\pmod q, \] then \(m/n\) is a sum of three positive unit fractions.

Put \(A=an\). Since \(ma\equiv1\pmod q\), the congruence on \(n\) gives \(A\equiv-e\pmod q\). Since \(e\mid a^2\) and \((a,q)=1\), also \((e,q)=1\). Define

\[ x=A,\qquad y=\frac{A+e}{q},\qquad z=\frac{A+A^2/e}{q}. \]

Since \(e\mid a^2\), the quantity \(A^2/e=(a^2/e)n^2\) is an integer. The congruence \(A\equiv-e\pmod q\) makes \(y\) integral; squaring and multiplying by the inverse of \(e\) modulo \(q\) gives \(A^2/e\equiv e\pmod q\), so \(z\) is integral too. Both are positive. Moreover

\[ qy-A=e,\qquad qz-A=A^2/e, \]

and hence \((qy-A)(qz-A)=A^2\). Expanding and cancelling \(A^2\) gives

\[ q^2yz-qA(y+z)=0. \]

Dividing by \(qyz\) yields \(q=A(1/y+1/z)\). Therefore

\[ \frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z=\frac{1+q}{an}=\frac m n. \]

Cubic divisor sums and pairwise coprimality

This section concerns the numerator \(4\). For \(a\ge1\), set

\[ \Lambda_{\mathrm{int}}(a)=\{h:h\mid a^2,\ h\notin\{1,a,a^2\}\}, \] \[ \Lambda_{<}(a)=\{h:h\mid a^2,\ 1<h<a\}. \]
Proposition 4.2 (cubic divisor sum). As \(L\to\infty\), \[ \sum_{a\le L}\frac{|\Lambda_{\mathrm{int}}(a)|}{4a-1}\sim\frac1{24\zeta(2)}(\log L)^3. \] Moreover, \[ \sum_{a\le L}\frac{|\Lambda_{<}(a)|}{4a-1}\sim\frac1{48\zeta(2)}(\log L)^3. \]

The involution \(h\mapsto a^2/h\) pairs divisors below and above \(a\). The excluded divisors \(1,a,a^2\) contribute only \(O(\log L)\) to the weighted sum.

For \(h\mid a^2\) put

\[ g=(a,h),\qquad d=h/g,\qquad A=a/g. \]

Then \((d,A)=1\), and \(h\mid a^2\) gives \(d\mid gA^2\). Hence \(d\mid g\); set \(f=g/d=g^2/h\). Now \(g=df\), so \(a=dfA\) and \(h=d^2f\). Conversely, any such decomposition with \((d,A)=1\) has \((a,h)=df\), which recovers \(d,f,A\) from these formulas and proves uniqueness.

Lemma 4.4 (divisor sum over primes). For \(L\ge3\), \[ \sum_{\substack{a\le L\\4a-1\ \mathrm{prime}}}\frac{\tau(a^2)}a\ll(\log L)^2. \]
Proposition 4.5 (pairwise coprime moduli). Let \(\mathcal B\subset\{a\le L\}\) be such that the integers \(4a-1\) with \(a\in\mathcal B\) are pairwise coprime. Then \[ \sum_{a\in\mathcal B}\frac{|\Lambda_{\mathrm{int}}(a)|}{4a-1}\ll(\log L)^2. \] Thus no subfamily of these congruences with pairwise coprime moduli can carry the cubic mass in Proposition 4.2.

Counting compatible congruences

Put

\[ z=(\log X)^4,\qquad U=(\log X)^8,\qquad P(z)=\prod_{\ell\le z}\ell. \]

The product is over primes. For each reduced class \(b\pmod{P(z)}\), let \(I_b\) be a finite family of congruences with squarefree moduli \(q_i\le X\), coprime to \(P(z)\), and residues \(c_i\pmod{q_i}\). Write \(w_i=1/q_i\) and \(\mu_b=\sum_iw_i\). Two indices are compatible when their residues agree modulo \((q_i,q_j)\); a set is compatible when every pair is. Put

\[ L_S=\operatorname{lcm}(q_i:i\in S),\qquad L_\varnothing=1,\qquad A(S)=\sum_{\substack{i\notin S\\S\cup\{i\}\ \mathrm{compatible}}}w_i(q_i,L_S). \]

Let \(\Omega_b\) be the finite product probability space over all primes appearing in the moduli \(q_i\). The event \(\mathcal A_i^{(b)}\) is defined by the residue \(c_i\pmod{q_i}\). If two events are compatible, the Chinese Remainder Theorem gives

\[ \mathbb P(\mathcal A_i^{(b)}\cap \mathcal A_j^{(b)})=\frac1{\operatorname{lcm}(q_i,q_j)}=\frac{(q_i,q_j)}{q_iq_j}; \]

if they are incompatible, the intersection is empty. Put

\[ W_b=\sum_{i\in I_b}\mathbf 1_{\mathcal A_i^{(b)}}. \]

For distinct events \(i\ne j\), let \(i\sim j\) denote \((q_i,q_j)>1\), and define

\[ \delta_b=\sup_i\sum_{\substack{j\sim i\\j\ne i}}\mathbb P(\mathcal A_j^{(b)}),\qquad \Delta_b=\sum_{\substack{\{i,j\}:i\sim j\\i\ne j}}\mathbb P(\mathcal A_i^{(b)}\cap \mathcal A_j^{(b)}). \]

Fix \(K\ge13\). In this section assume, uniformly in \(b\), that \(\mu_b\to\infty\) as \(X\to\infty\) and

\[ A(S)\le2\mu_b\quad(|S|\le2K\mu_b,\ S\ \mathrm{compatible}),\qquad \delta_b\le\frac14,\qquad \Delta_b=o(\mu_b). \]
Theorem 5.2 (probability of no event). Uniformly in \(b\), \[ \mathbb P(W_b=0)\le\exp\{-(1-o(1))\mu_b\}. \]

We give a conditioning proof related to Spencer's argument. Fix \(b\) and abbreviate \(\mathcal A_i^{(b)}\), \(\mu_b\), \(\delta_b\), \(\Delta_b\) by \(A_i\), \(\mu\), \(\delta\), \(\Delta\). Put \(B_S=\bigcap_{j\in S}A_j^c\). We first show by induction on \(|S|\) that

\[ \mathbb P(A_i\mid B_S)\le2w_i\qquad(i\notin S). \]

Write \(\Gamma=\{j\in S:j\sim i\}\) and \(T=S\setminus\Gamma\). If \(\Gamma\) is empty, independence gives equality with \(w_i\). Otherwise \(|T|<|S|\), so induction and the union bound give

\[ \mathbb P(B_\Gamma\mid B_T)\ge1-\sum_{j\in\Gamma}\mathbb P(A_j\mid B_T)\ge1-2\delta. \]

Since \(A_i\) is independent of \(B_T\), division by this lower bound proves \(\mathbb P(A_i\mid B_S)\le w_i/(1-2\delta)\le2w_i\).

For a lower bound, the same partition gives

\[ \mathbb P(A_i\mid B_S)\ge\mathbb P(A_i\cap B_\Gamma\mid B_T)\ge w_i-\sum_{j\in\Gamma}\mathbb P(A_i\cap A_j\mid B_T). \]

Fix \(j\in\Gamma\), put \(E=\{k\in T:k\sim j\}\), and write \(T_0=T\setminus E\). No vertex of \(T_0\) is adjacent to either \(i\) or \(j\), so the pair \((A_i,A_j)\) is independent of \(B_{T_0}\). The bound just proved gives \(\mathbb P(B_E\mid B_{T_0})\ge1-2\delta\), and hence

\[ \mathbb P(A_i\cap A_j\mid B_T)\le\frac{\mathbb P(A_i\cap A_j\mid B_{T_0})}{\mathbb P(B_E\mid B_{T_0})}\le\frac{\mathbb P(A_i\cap A_j)}{1-2\delta}. \]

Order the vertices and apply the lower bound with \(S\) equal to the preceding vertices, so that each unordered edge contributes once. The chain rule, \(1-t\le e^{-t}\), and these uniform bounds give

\[ \mathbb P(W_b=0)\le\exp\left\{-\mu+\frac{\Delta}{1-2\delta}\right\}\le\exp\{-\mu+2\Delta\}=\exp\{-(1-o(1))\mu_b\}. \]
Theorem 5.4 (finite counting estimate). Fix \(K\ge13\) and put \(R_b=\lfloor K\mu_b\rfloor\). Suppose that \[ P(z)X^{2R_b}\exp\{2\mu_b\}\le N\exp\{-4\mu_b\}. \] Then, uniformly in \(b\), \[ \#\{n\le N:n\equiv b\pmod{P(z)},\quad n\notin \mathcal A_i^{(b)}\ \text{for every }i\in I_b\}\le\frac{N}{P(z)}\exp\{-(1-o(1))\mu_b\}. \]
Lemma 5.5 (lifting). Let \(\mathcal E\) be a set of positive integers closed under taking divisors. Suppose \(2\le y\ll\log N\) and, uniformly for \(\sqrt N\le T\le N\), \[ \#\{u\le T:u\in\mathcal E,\ (u,P(y))=1\}\le ATe^{-F} \] for \(A>0\) and \(F\ge0\). Then \[ \#(\mathcal E\cap[1,N])\ll AN\log(2y)e^{-F}+N^{3/4+o(1)}. \] The error is uniform for a fixed constant in \(y\ll\log N\).

The selected family

Fix \(m\ge4\) for the rest of the proof; all thresholds may depend on \(m\). Fix

\[ (\eta,\sigma,\lambda,\theta,\beta,\nu)=\left(\frac1{100},\frac1{20},\frac1{10},\frac3{10},\frac23,\frac8{25}\right), \] \[ S_0=X^\eta,\qquad Y_0=X^\lambda,\qquad Y=X^\sigma,\qquad H=X^\theta. \]

Define \(\mathcal F\) to be the set of integers \(e=rs^2\), \(\operatorname{rad}(e)=rs\), where \(r\) and \(s\) are squarefree, \((r,s)=1\), \((rs,m)=1\), and

\[ 1\le s\le S_0,\qquad \frac{Y_0}{s}<r\le\frac{H}{s^2}. \]

We first fix a reduced residue class modulo \(P(z)\): \(b\in(\mathbb Z/P(z)\mathbb Z)^\times\). For \(e\in\mathcal F\), choose a small squarefree odd divisor \(d_-\) and a rough squarefree cofactor \(d_+\) such that

\[ d_-\le U,\qquad d_-\mid P(z),\qquad b\equiv-me\pmod{d_-}, \] \[ d_-d_+\le Y,\qquad (d_+,P(z))=1,\qquad (d_-d_+,m\operatorname{rad}(e))=1. \]

Finally choose a prime \(p\) satisfying

\[ X^\beta<p\le X^{1-\sigma},\qquad d_-d_+p\equiv-1\pmod{m\operatorname{rad}(e)}. \]

For every quadruple \(i=(e,d_-,d_+,p)\) satisfying these conditions, define

\[ Q_i=d_-d_+p,\qquad q_i=d_+p,\qquad a_i=\frac{Q_i+1}{m}, \] \[ \mathcal A_i^{(b)}=\{n:n\equiv-me\pmod{q_i}\},\qquad w_i^{(b)}=\frac1{q_i}. \]

The full modulus is \(Q_i\); after conditioning on \(n\equiv b\pmod{P(z)}\), only the residual modulus \(q_i\) contributes to the density. Indeed, \(d_-\mid P(z)\) and \(b\equiv-me\pmod{d_-}\) give \(n\equiv-me\pmod{d_-}\). Combining this with \(\mathcal A_i^{(b)}\) gives

\[ n\equiv-me\pmod{Q_i}. \]

The congruence on \(p\) implies that \(a_i\) is an integer and \(\operatorname{rad}(e)\mid a_i\). Because \(e=rs^2\) and \(\operatorname{rad}(e)=rs\), this gives \(e\mid a_i^2\); also \(Q_i=ma_i-1\). Lemma 2.3 therefore gives a representation. Let \(I_b\) be the set of all quadruples above and set

\[ \mu_b=\sum_{i\in I_b}w_i^{(b)}. \]
Theorem 3.7 (Bombieri–Vinogradov). For every fixed \(A>0\) and \(0<\vartheta<1/2\), uniformly for \(x\ge3\), \[ \sum_{q\le x^\vartheta}\max_{(a,q)=1}\left|\pi(x;q,a)-\frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(q)}\right|\ll_{A,\vartheta}\frac{x}{(\log x)^A}. \]
Proposition 6.2 (quadratic divisor sums). The selected divisor family, before any sieving at small primes, satisfies \[ M_1:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal F}\frac1{\operatorname{rad}(e)}\asymp_m(\log X)^2,\qquad M_\varphi:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal F}\frac1{\varphi(\operatorname{rad}(e))}\asymp_m(\log X)^2. \]
Lemma 6.5 (average over small divisors). Uniformly for every \(b\in(\mathbb Z/P(z)\mathbb Z)^\times\), \[ \Sigma_b:=\sum_{\substack{d_-\le U\\d_-\mid P(z)\\d_-\ \mathrm{odd}}}\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathcal F\\b\equiv-me\pmod{d_-}}}\frac1{\varphi(m\operatorname{rad}(e))}\asymp_m(\log X)^2\log z. \]
Proposition 6.6 (total event weight). Uniformly in \(b\in(\mathbb Z/P(z)\mathbb Z)^\times\), \[ \mu_b\asymp_m(\log X)^3. \]
Lemma 6.8 (uniqueness when the large prime is shared). Two distinct compatible events in \(I_b\) cannot share the same large prime \(p\). For any prime \(p>X^\beta\), the total weight of events having this large prime is at most \(H/p\le X^{\theta-\beta}=o(1)\).
Lemma 6.9 (overlap bounds). Uniformly in \(b\), \[ \delta_b=o_m(1),\qquad \Delta_b=o_m(\mu_b). \] For every fixed \(K>0\) and every compatible \(S\) with \(|S|\le K\mu_b\), \[ A(S)\le\mu_b(1+o_{m,K}(1)). \]

Completion of the proof

Proof of Theorem 2. Fix \(m\ge4\) and \(K=13\). Proposition 6.6 and Lemma 6.9, using the latter with parameter \(2K\), verify the hypotheses of Section 5 for all sufficiently large \(X\), uniformly in \(b\). Choose

\[ \log X=\alpha_m(\log N)^{1/4}, \]

with \(\alpha_m>0\) sufficiently small. Lemma 3.1 gives \(\log P(z)\ll z=(\log X)^4\). For \(R_b=\lfloor K\mu_b\rfloor\),

\[ \log P(z)+2R_b\log X+6\mu_b\ll_m(\log X)^4. \]

Taking \(\alpha_m\) small makes this at most \(\tfrac12\log N\). Since \(\log T\ge\tfrac12\log N\) for \(\sqrt N\le T\le N\), this choice of \(X\), cutoff, and family satisfies the level condition of Theorem 5.4 throughout that range. Theorem 5.4, summed over the reduced classes modulo \(P(z)\), gives

\[ \#\{n\le T:(n,P(z))=1,\ m/n\text{ has no representation}\}\ll_m T\exp\{-c'_m(\log N)^{3/4}\}. \]

The exceptional set is closed under taking divisors: a representation for a divisor of \(n\) scales to one for \(n\). Apply Lemma 5.5 at the common cutoff \(z=\alpha_m^4\log N\). The factor \(\log(2z)\) and the error \(N^{3/4+o_m(1)}\) are absorbed by decreasing \(c'_m\).

Proof of Theorem 1. Take \(m=4\) in Theorem 2 and use \(E(N)\le E_4(N)\). The assertion for prime denominators follows as well, since \(\pi(N)\gg N/\log N\) and the factor \(\log N\) is absorbed by decreasing the constant in the exponential.

Further remarks

Remark 7.1 (geometry). For numerator \(4\), the surface \(U_n:4xyz=n(xy+xz+yz)\) is an affine part of a Cayley cubic. In projective coordinates \((x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3)\), its closure is \[ 4x_1x_2x_3=nx_0(x_1x_2+x_1x_3+x_2x_3). \] Fixing \(x=an\) gives \((4a-1)yz=an(y+z)\), so the projective closures of these curves are the conic components of explicit plane sections.
Remark 7.2 (effectivity). The thresholds obtained here inherit the ineffectivity of the usual Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem. Johnston gives effective variants for sifting problems; applying them to this family requires a separate analysis.
